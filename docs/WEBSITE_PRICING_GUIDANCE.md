# Website Pricing Guidance — Diqto Voice

_Last updated: 2026-07-05_

## Core website message

Sell the business outcome, not the implementation detail:

> Speak French. Your client hears English. No install for them. Works across Teams, Zoom, Meet, and Webex.

Avoid leading with “AI audio tokens”, “OpenAI Realtime”, or implementation internals. Mention AI only as support for the user outcome.

## Value-vs-cost packaging thesis

Use the **Diqto Voice Hours** concept on pricing and beta pages:

- subscription gives professional access plus included live translated voice hours;
- usage is bounded and transparent;
- overage is explicit, not hidden;
- no unlimited plan at launch;
- team plans pool included hours;
- advanced meeting intelligence increases value without implying unlimited live audio.

Recommended public wording:

> Plans include Diqto Voice Hours — live translated voice capacity for your business calls. You can add extra hours when needed.

Avoid wording that implies raw meeting duration if the backend meters useful active translated speech.

## Initial price anchors

These are strategic anchors, not final public copy:

| Plan | Anchor | Included usage | Site framing |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Beta / Early Access | €19/mo | 3h | limited early-access plan |
| Solo | €29/mo HT | 5h | for independents and founders |
| Pro | €59/mo HT | 15h | for regular international calls |
| Team | €39/seat/mo HT | 8h/seat pooled | for small B2B teams |
| Overage | €8–10/h | additional usage | add hours when needed |
| Enterprise | custom | committed volume | compliance, admin, SLA |

For France/consumer-facing pages, be careful with TTC vs HT. Early B2B pages should prefer **HT** wording.

## Copy guardrails

Do say:

- “No install for your client.”
- “Works with your existing meeting app.”
- “Mac beta first.”
- “Included live translated voice hours.”
- “Add extra hours when needed.”
- “Transcript, summaries, and follow-up help you capture meeting value.”

Do not say yet:

- “Unlimited translation.”
- “Fully autonomous interpreter.”
- “Works perfectly in every meeting app.”
- “No setup required.”
- “Human interpreter quality guaranteed.”
- “Realtime-2 powered” or model-specific marketing.

## Pricing page structure

1. Outcome hero: “Speak your language. They hear theirs.”
2. Three paid cards: Solo, Pro, Team.
3. Enterprise CTA.
4. Explanation of Diqto Voice Hours.
5. “No install for your client” proof block.
6. Mac beta limitations and supported meeting apps.
7. FAQ: minutes, overage, privacy, languages, setup, refunds.

## RevenueCat / checkout implication

RevenueCat can drive offerings, paywalls, and entitlement state, but the site should not promise usage behavior that only RevenueCat can enforce. The Diqto backend owns:

- included hour ledger;
- active usage accounting;
- overage consent;
- session minting allowance;
- plan-to-feature rules.

The website can still present plans as simple subscription cards while the backend protects margin.
